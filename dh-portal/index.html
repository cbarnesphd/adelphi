Conceptual Framework

Why Bring Digital Humanities into the Classroom?

Digital Humanities (DH) brings computational methods and digital publishing to the questions humanists have always asked about literature, history, philosophy, and art. Alongside close reading and archival research, it adds ways of modeling, mapping, visualizing, and sharing evidence, and it asks students not only to write about scholarship but to make it public.

Distant Reading & Text Mining

Instead of reading one novel at a time, scholars analyze thousands of texts simultaneously to discover subtle shifts in syntax, genre patterns, or sentiment across centuries.

Spatial History & Deep Mapping

GIS layers enable students to map historic events against demographic, political, and environmental data, uncovering how geography shapes cultural narrative.

3D Virtual Heritage & Networks

Reconstruction of lost monuments, social network graphing of historical correspondents, and crowdsourced archival preservation democratize access to memory.

Pedagogical Infrastructure

Most Common DH Skills, Tools & Platforms

A syllabus-ready breakdown of software by learning curve and entry barrier. Availability at Adelphi is noted where known; see IT's standard software list for lab installations.

Entry-Level / No Code

Web-Based Exploration

Ideal for 100/200 level humanities courses requiring no installation or code preparation.

  • Voyant Tools: Web text mining and word cloud visualizations.
  • StoryMapJS (Knight Lab): Free narrative maps built from a Google Sheet. (ArcGIS StoryMaps needs an ArcGIS Online account; ask IT whether Adelphi's Esri license includes one.)
  • KnightLab TimelineJS: Interactive media timelines from Google Sheets.
  • Mapping Paintings: Map the provenance and movement of artworks from a spreadsheet, no GIS required (mappingpaintings.org).
Intermediate / GUI Tools

Archival Work, Data Cleaning & Visual Analytics

Desktop applications and specialized platforms for upper-level majors and research projects.

  • Omeka Classic / S: Museum-grade digital exhibit and metadata publishing.
  • Tropy: Free desktop tool (from RRCHNM) for organizing, describing, and transcribing research photographs of archival documents.
  • OpenRefine: Free tool for cleaning messy tabular data and reconciling names against Wikidata before mapping or analysis.
  • Transkribus: Handwritten-text recognition for manuscripts, as used by Transcribe Bentham; free to start, with paid credits for large batches.
  • QGIS: Free, open-source GIS for any laptop. ArcGIS Pro is also installed in the Swirbul Information Commons and the SCB 27 and 227 labs.
Advanced / Scripting

Computational Methods

For capstone, honors, or graduate-level research in data-driven humanities.

  • Python (spaCy / NLTK) and R Studio: Topic modeling, NER, and text statistics; both are installed in Adelphi computer labs.
  • Data analysis (pandas / R tidyverse, Jupyter): Cleaning, joining, and analyzing historical datasets such as census tables, voyage records, or performance histories; SPSS is also available in Adelphi labs for statistical work.
  • VS Code with the Scholarly XML extension: Free TEI markup and schema validation (Oxygen XML Editor, the commercial standard, is not part of Adelphi's software list).
  • Blender / Three.js: Photogrammetry processing and 3D web rendering.
  • Gephi / NetworkX: Network graph layout, centrality metrics, and community detection from cleaned relational data.
All Levels / Every Tool

Generative AI in DH Coursework

Generative AI is part of the toolkit this portal and the Digital Publishing track describe. Students use it the way they use any other tool: openly, with the sources checked, and with the work documented.

  • Adelphi's licensed tools: Microsoft Copilot and Google Gemini are covered by the University's enterprise licenses; prompts and uploads are not used to train the models, which matters when students work with unpublished archival material. See IT's Generative AI page.
  • Specify and verify: Students state what they asked the tool to do, check every output against primary and scholarly sources, and keep a record of prompts and revisions as part of the project's documentation.
  • AI-use statement: Every published project carries a short statement of what AI was used for, what it was not used for, and how the results were checked, alongside the project's methodology note and license.
  • Where it helps: First-pass OCR and transcription cleanup, reshaping spreadsheets for a map or timeline, drafting and debugging small scripts, and performing quality control checks. The interpretive work, the argument, and responsibility for the result stay with the student.

Career Pathways

Translating DH Skills to Post-College Careers

How training in digital humanities provides humanities graduates with market-ready, high-growth technical competencies without sacrificing critical thinking.

Data Analyst / Policy Researcher

DH Skill: Distant Reading & Data Cleaning

Students learn to clean messy historical datasets, execute queries, and synthesize trends—directly applicable to government policy and corporate intelligence.

UX Research & Information Architecture

DH Skill: Metadata Curation & Archiving

Creating Dublin Core metadata schemas teaches students user navigation pathways, taxonomy building, and intuitive web design principles.

GIS & Urban Planning Specialist

DH Skill: Spatial Mapping & Historical GIS

Mapping historical real estate redlining or urban density equips students with geospatial problem-solving skills sought by regional planning bodies.

Digital Asset Manager & Public Historian

DH Skill: TEI-XML & Digital Archiving

Transcribing and encoding cultural materials provides direct training for careers in libraries, museums, digital publishing, and non-profit archives.
